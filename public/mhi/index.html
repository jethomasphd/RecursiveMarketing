Why a job board just asked you to play a game

You came here to search for a job. We asked you to pause first. That might seem strange. Here's why we did it, and why the science says it matters.

The problem we're trying to solve

Job searching is one of the most stressful experiences in modern life. It combines uncertainty, rejection, financial pressure, and identity threat into a single sustained activity. Studies link prolonged unemployment and job searching to increased rates of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation.

Traditional job boards do nothing about this. They optimize for clicks and applications — not for the human being sitting at the screen. We wanted to try something different.

The idea: micro-interventions at the moment of need

In 2024, former NIMH Director Dr. Thomas Insel wrote about a powerful but underappreciated pattern in mental health research: tiny interventions, delivered at the right moment, can produce outsized effects on millions of people.

"Small interventions can interrupt destructive patterns before they settle into place." — Thomas Insel, "A Video Game, a Postcard, and a Grandmother"

Insel documented a series of studies showing that brief, simple, and scalable encounters — some lasting only minutes — produced clinical outcomes that rival or exceed expensive, long-term treatments. The key insight: timing and context matter more than duration.

The evidence base

Study 1 · The Lancet Psychiatry
Tetris and Trauma Recovery
A randomized controlled trial found that playing Tetris for 20 minutes while recalling traumatic memories reduced intrusive thoughts by approximately 70% compared to control groups. The mechanism: visual-spatial engagement competes with the brain's ability to replay distressing imagery. The game literally blocks the rumination loop.
Result: ~70% reduction in intrusive thoughts
Study 2 · Shandong University
Hearing Aids and Depression
Providing hearing aids to older adults with hearing loss produced mood improvements "surpassing anything" documented in antidepressant medication studies. A simple correction of sensory isolation was more powerful than pharmacology.
Result: Mood improvement exceeding antidepressant benchmarks
Study 3 · 80,000+ participants
Digital Hypnosis and Stress
A single app-delivered hypnosis session significantly reduced stress across a massive sample. One session. One app. Measurable change.
Result: Significant stress reduction from a single session
Study 4 · JAMA · Randomized Trial
The Friendship Bench (Zimbabwe)
Trained grandmothers delivering structured problem-solving therapy on wooden benches in community settings achieved profound reductions in depression and anxiety. No clinics. No prescriptions. Grandmothers on benches, listening and guiding.
Result: Profound reductions in depression and anxiety
Study 5 · Decades of follow-up
Postcards and Suicide Prevention
Psychiatrist Jerome Motto found that sending periodic check-in postcards — simple "thinking of you" notes — to people who had been hospitalized for suicidal ideation measurably reduced subsequent suicide rates. The cost: a stamp and five seconds of someone's time.
Result: Measurable reduction in suicide rates
Research Program · Jessica Schleider, PhD
"Little Treatments, Big Effects"
Psychologist Jessica Schleider has built an entire research program around "micro-interventions" — brief digital exercises designed to shift thinking patterns. Her work, documented in her book Little Treatments, Big Effects, shows that single-session interventions can produce lasting change in youth mental health.
Result: Lasting change from single-session digital interventions

What we built and why

The 15-second game you just played (or were offered) combines three evidence-based mechanisms into a single micro-intervention:

1. Visual-spatial engagement (the Tetris principle). Guiding falling blocks demands just enough cognitive attention to interrupt the anxiety loop — the racing thoughts about rejections, deadlines, and bills that build up during a job search.

2. Paced breathing. The blocks fall at a rhythm synchronized to a calm breathing pattern: 4 seconds in, 4 seconds out. Controlled breathing is one of the most robust findings in stress physiology — it activates the parasympathetic nervous system and reduces cortisol within minutes.

3. Brief positive messaging (the postcard principle). As rows complete, gentle words appear: "breathe," "you're here," "you matter." These aren't platitudes — they're calibrated micro-affirmations delivered at a moment of accomplishment within the game, anchoring a small positive association.

Why a job board?

Because this is where people are when they need it most.

The entire insight of Insel's analysis is that interventions work best when delivered at the moment of need, through channels people are already using. A job board is a point of maximum stress for a vulnerable population. Every person who lands on this page is, by definition, in a moment of transition, uncertainty, and often distress.

We're not a therapy app. We're not claiming to treat depression. We're saying: if a postcard can reduce suicide rates, and Tetris can reduce trauma flashbacks, then maybe a 15-second pause before a job search can make the difference between someone who spirals and someone who takes a breath and keeps going.

What we don't do

We don't collect data about your mental health. We don't track your game performance. We don't sell your information. We don't claim this replaces professional help. The game is entirely client-side — it runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

If you're in crisis, please contact the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline by calling or texting 988. It's free, confidential, and available 24/7.

The bigger picture

This is an experiment in what happens when technology takes the side of the person using it. Not the advertiser. Not the employer. Not the platform. You.

Insel's argument is that the next frontier in mental health isn't just better medications or better therapies — it's better delivery. Scalable interventions pushed through digital channels to millions of people at the exact moment they need them. A video game, a postcard, a grandmother on a bench.

This is a job board that took that idea seriously. That's all.
